# A preset's hook

This file is for a whole pack whose `style.md` has a `## Hook` section, as the [reference style skill](../SKILL.md#a-presets-hook) says. Its hook was matched once, when the preset was built, so an edit copies it rather than matching it again.

The section's table holds the reference's hook one row per beat: its length, whether it shows the speaker, footage or a graphic, what it shows, the role of what it says (a number, a brand, a statement, a question, a list, a proof image), how it enters and leaves, its caption treatment and how many words that treatment sets, and what it is built from. Map it onto the user's opening in a working file beside the caption rows before you plan any treatment of your own there: a punch-in, a reframe or an image of yours over the user's opening goes only on a beat the table gives the speaker.

- Once the cut is final, map the table onto the user's opening claim, its first sentence or two, placed by what is said rather than by the reference's seconds. The hook's signature graphic, its first graphic beat, is kept on the user's opening line with its entrance, matched as built. The rest of the opening follows the user's content: keep a row where the user's words carry its role, in the table's order where those words allow, the user's sentences deciding where each beat changes, a beat changing at a phrase boundary when a sentence runs longer than it. A beat whose caption treatment builds words into one stack, as its row names, carries the whole phrase that stack shows, as long as the phrase takes: it never ends between two words of one phrase, so the stack sets as many words as its row says. Add to each row you keep the user's words that carry it and their times; those rows open the [beat plan](../SKILL.md#plan-the-beats).
- A role decides what a beat carries. Each beat you keep carries the user's content of the same role: a number becomes a counter on the user's number or quantity, a brand a card of the user's own brand, a statement the big type of the user's line, a proof image an image the user supplied or researched imagery, and with neither, the live speaker clip in the beat's frame, never a still of the user's footage, as the aesthetic rules' [material section](../../aip/references/aesthetic-rules.md#1-material-real-things-as-real-imagery-concepts-as-animation) says. A beat whose role the user's words lack is dropped, never forced onto words that carry something else, and the user's content decides what that stretch shows.
- A graphic beat you keep copies the template its row names, as the [style pack](style-pack.md) says, set to the beat's length and to the times of the user's words through the timing the template names, then `compare`d once over a frame of the user's footage to see that it reads there. The template sets the beat's look and timing, and its subject slot takes what the user's line names, drawn in that look and arranged as that subject needs, as the Motion graphics item of [What to copy](../SKILL.md#what-to-copy) says. A template the row marks unresolved is the closest the preset came, and is copied the same way.
- A footage beat moves when the reference's does. A row marked moving plays moving footage: a stretch of the user's own recording where the speaker is off camera, such as the product or place they filmed, as a root video clip on its own track (`src="public/source.mp4"` with its `data-media-start`, as the [composition contract](../../aip-composition/SKILL.md) says for footage), or a researched clip the same way, with the row's tint drawn by a moment over it, a translucent ground. Only a row marked still takes a still image, one the user supplied or a researched one, under a push; several stills in a row read as a slideshow, so at most one beat of the hook is still. A footage beat with no such clip or image shows the speaker live, never a still of the user's footage and never the speaker from another moment, talking or silent, as the aesthetic rules' [material section](../../aip/references/aesthetic-rules.md#1-material-real-things-as-real-imagery-concepts-as-animation) says. That holds when a row's `built from` names the user's recording at another moment or a frame of the user's footage: such wording predates this rule. Compare the beat once against its keyframe.
