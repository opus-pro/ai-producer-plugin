# Source supporting visuals

Once preparation has started, plan in the [creative direction](../SKILL.md#creative-direction) and research supporting material in the initial plan. This research runs on every new edit: real imagery is part of the default, never an alternative to motion graphics. Identify transcript beats that need real products, organizations, interfaces, devices, places, people, or event imagery or footage. Use suitable supplied assets first; respect requests to avoid external sources.

- When the user supplied B-roll, review it as [supplied B-roll](supplied-broll.md) says before deciding coverage.
- An image or clip the user says they will send later is theirs to give: until it arrives, its beat shows the speaker or an authored graphic of what the line says, never a stand-in taken from their footage (the aesthetic rules' [material section](aesthetic-rules.md#1-material-real-things-as-real-imagery-concepts-as-animation)), and the reply that hands the project back names, by time, each moment still waiting for it.
- A-roll follows the settled speech cut; fit B-roll to it without recutting speech to accommodate an asset's length. Trim irrelevant head or tail, adjust coverage over related speech, choose another excerpt, or return to the presenter when a clip does not fit. Let useful matches and continuity decide coverage, without a fixed clip count or coverage quota. Keep a concise local mapping of speech beats, source in/out times, and cut placements.
- For missing material, search the Web yourself for images and video clips, whichever shows the beat better: a clip when the beat is an action, a place or a process, an image when it is one thing to look at. Open official or primary source pages first, press kits and media rooms among them, then attribution-free libraries first: Pexels, Pixabay, Unsplash and CC0 / public-domain sources for images, and Pexels and Pixabay for video. Fall back to Wikimedia Commons, Openverse and the Internet Archive (preferring CC0 or public-domain files, and commercial-reuse terms with no NC or ND) only when attribution-free libraries lack the subject; if a fallback file requires attribution, keep its source page, author, and license in a local off-screen note only, and never render license notices (such as CC BY), author credits or library names onto the video. Never take a clip from a platform's player such as YouTube, Instagram or TikTok. Search in batches by beat: one batch covering every beat that needs real imagery, plus at most one follow-up batch for unresolved beats.
- Trim a clip to the stretch you use and convert it to H.264 `.mp4` no larger than the canvas before staging it. An export handles only so many distinct video files: when a run of beats needs footage from many files with no speaker beat between them, join the excerpts locally into one reel file and reference that. A download the host refuses moves on to the next candidate, and then to an authored graphic. Before authoring, inspect a downsized image sheet or representative source frames for subject match, usable detail, and crop suitability. Replace unsuitable candidates within the research limit.
- Use accepted assets in relevant beats with framing or motion that explains the speech. Unless the user names a trigger or a count, use each image or single-shot clip once at its best-fitting beat. A long video with multiple relevant moments can supply several distinct excerpts across the edit; do not restrict the source file to one use or repeat the same excerpt by default. A stated trigger such as "whenever X is mentioned" applies at every matching beat. Apply the [on-screen text rules](../SKILL.md#on-screen-text) to visible labels.
