# Captions for an AI Producer project

Captions are moments you write, the same way as any other motion graphic: one visual moment per stretch of speech, each a composition under the [composition contract](../aip-composition/SKILL.md) whose cues are static text elements shown and hidden on the moment's own paused timeline. Static text in a moment is what the editor lets the user select, retype, recolor and change the font of, so every word a caption shows is written in the composition's HTML, never generated by its script. Never call `build_captions`, never use the `narrator-captions` id, and never write `compositions/narrator_captions.html` or `compositions/transcript-src.json`: those are the service's own caption layer, which this skill does not use.

## Timing

Write the captions once the speaker cut is final. Run [caption_cues.py](../aip/scripts/caption_cues.py) on the render-engine directory:

```sh
python3 <loaded-aip-skill>/scripts/caption_cues.py <render-engine-root> --max-words 4
```

It synchronizes `compositions/editing-script.json` with the cut in `index.html`, as preflight's `--sync-editing-script` does, so that document goes in the same upload, and prints one row per caption moment: `id`, `src`, `start` on the cut, `duration`, `src_anchor` (the source second at `start`), and `cues`, each with `in`, `out` and its `words` as `[text, in, out]`, in seconds from the moment's start. Take every time from it, never from arithmetic of your own on `get_transcript`. A moment never spans a speaker cut, a sentence end or a long pause, so when the user later cuts between two moments in the editor, each moves whole with its source anchor. A cut inside a moment leaves its later words early, and one that removes its first word hides the whole moment, because the editor hides a moment whose anchor is cut away; either way the moment is right again once you write it afresh for the new cut. `--max-words` is how many words a cue shows at once: 4 by default, 1 for a look that shows one word at a time, and 8 to 12 for speech whose transcript words are a character or two each, such as Chinese or Japanese. You may merge two cues or split one at a word boundary, using the words' own times, and correct a word's spelling, casing or punctuation; never change what was said.

Mount each row as a moment host, after every other moment inside the root and all on one track no other moment uses, so the captions draw above every moment:

```html
<div class="visual-host clip" data-composition-id="captions-01" data-title="Captions 1"
  data-composition-src="compositions/captions-01.html" data-start="<start>" data-duration="<duration>"
  data-src-anchor="<src_anchor>" data-track-index="<n>" data-width="W" data-height="H"></div>
```

Publish the caption moments with their hosts in `index.html`, the synchronized EditingScript and the font files. One commit carries at most 200 files and at most 256 KiB of inline `content` in all, so a long video's captions go out over several commits in timeline order: each mounts only the caption hosts whose files it or an earlier commit carried, and only the last one closes authoring.

The EditingScript sync refuses a cut change while caption moments are mounted (`editing_script_mounted_captions_require_rebake`), because their timing belongs to the old cut. To recut a captioned project, take the caption hosts out of `index.html` and drop their files from the next commit, make the cut, run the script again and write the captions afresh. A project whose captions the service built earlier, a `narrator-captions` host, keeps them until the user asks to change its captions or its cut; then replace that layer the same way.

## A caption moment

```html
<template>
  <div data-composition-id="captions-01" data-width="1080" data-height="1920">
    <style>
      @font-face { font-family: "Inter"; font-weight: 700; src: url("public/fonts/Inter-700.woff2") format("woff2"); }
      [data-composition-id="captions-01"] .cue {
        position: absolute; left: 8%; right: 12%; top: 70%; transform: translateY(-50%);
        text-align: center; font: 700 64px/1.15 "Inter"; color: #ffffff;
        text-shadow: 0 3px 12px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.6); visibility: hidden;
      }
    </style>
    <div class="cue" data-in="0" data-out="0.82">What do you like</div>
    <div class="cue" data-in="0.82" data-out="1.9">about using Opus</div>
    <script>
      const root = document.querySelector('[data-composition-id="captions-01"]');
      const tl = gsap.timeline({ paused: true });
      root.querySelectorAll(".cue").forEach((cue) => {
        tl.set(cue, { visibility: "visible" }, Number(cue.dataset.in));
        tl.set(cue, { visibility: "hidden" }, Number(cue.dataset.out));
      });
      window.__timelines["captions-01"] = tl;
    </script>
  </div>
</template>
```

- One cue element per cue, its text written in the HTML, its `data-in` and `data-out` from the script's row. The timeline reads them, so retiming a cue never touches its text, and a cue's entrance or exit tween starts at its `data-in` or ends at its `data-out`. A look that treats words one by one wraps each word in its own span, with that word's times, inside the cue.
- One cue on screen at a time: the next appears as the previous leaves.
- Each caption file is at most 64 KiB, a moment's own. Repeat the `@font-face` and the cue rules in every caption file, so each moment renders on its own.
- A caption moment is not a beat: it takes no sound effect, and it does not count toward the visual moments of the fine cut.

## The default look

Without a reference or the user's words about it, every caption is the same plain look: Inter Bold, white, with a soft dark shadow, centred, at about 6% of the canvas's short side (64 px on a 1080-wide canvas), at most two lines, with no emphasized word. Its band is centred at 70% of the frame height on a portrait canvas and at 78% on a landscape or square one while the speaker owns the frame; under a visual moment it sits where the [framing skill](../aip-framing/SKILL.md) puts the caption for that moment's layout, set on each cue that plays there.

Download the font from Google Fonts or its Fontsource mirror and stage it under `public/fonts/`, in the same upload as the first caption file. Stage every file the captions' characters need, and for speech in a script Inter lacks, such as Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic or Hindi, take the Noto Sans family for that script instead. Never name a font you have not staged: the export renders on a machine with its own fonts, so an unstaged name renders there as another face.

## With a reference

When the user's edit copies a reference, the [reference style skill](../aip-reference-style/SKILL.md) decides the caption look, which cues show together and how they are laid out; it replaces the default look wholesale. This skill's timing, mounting and legibility still apply.

## What keeps captions legible

These hold over the default, a reference and the user's words:

- Every word stays wholly inside the frame's safe area for its whole time on screen, at its largest scale in any entrance or emphasis: the safe area the [aesthetic rules](../aip/references/aesthetic-rules.md) set for the canvas, clear of a portrait canvas's platform buttons on the right edge and its caption bar at the bottom.
- A cue wraps inside its box rather than shrinking; split a cue that would take a third line instead of making its type smaller.
- No cue plays under a `headline` overlay or a `statement` full cover, which already draw the phrase, under a logo with text or a quote card, which the aesthetic rules give their beat alone, or under a full cover whose detail must occupy the band; drop those cues, and the whole moment when every cue falls there.
