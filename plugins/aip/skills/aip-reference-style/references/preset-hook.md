# A preset's hook

This file is for a whole pack whose `style.md` has a `## Hook` section, as the [reference style skill](../SKILL.md#a-presets-hook) says. Its hook was matched once, when the preset was built, so an edit copies it rather than matching it again.

The section's table holds the reference's hook one row per beat: its length, whether it shows the speaker, footage or a graphic, what it shows, the role of what it says (a number, a brand, a statement, a question, a list, a proof image), how it enters and leaves, its caption treatment and how many words that treatment sets, and what it is built from. Map it onto the user's opening in a working file beside the caption rows before you plan any treatment of your own there: a punch-in, a reframe or an image of yours over the user's opening goes only on a beat the table gives the speaker.

- Once the cut is final, map the table onto the user's opening claim, its first sentence or two, placed by what is said rather than by the reference's seconds: one row for each of its rows and in its order, about the same length each, the user's sentences deciding where each beat changes but never which beats appear, a beat changing at a phrase boundary when a sentence runs longer than it. A beat whose caption treatment builds words into one stack, as its row names, carries the whole phrase that stack shows, as long as the phrase takes: it never ends between two words of one phrase, so the stack sets as many words as its row says. Add to each row the user's words that carry it and their times. Then check the mapping against the table: as many rows, in the same order, the speaker only where the reference shows the speaker, and about the same share of graphics. A mapping that differs is written again.
- The order comes from the table, and a role decides only what a beat carries: a beat is never moved to reach the user's content of its role. Each beat carries the user's content of the same role at its place: a number becomes a counter on the user's number or quantity, a brand a card of the user's own brand, a statement the big type of the user's line, a proof image the user's own frame or researched imagery. When the user's words hold nothing of a beat's role, the beat keeps its look, its length and its place over the nearest content the user has, its picture drawn for that content; a beat is never dropped for want of the reference's content.
- A graphic beat copies the template its row names, as the [style pack](style-pack.md) says, set to the beat's length and to the times of the user's words through the timing the template names, then `compare`d once over a frame of the user's footage to see that it reads there. The template sets the beat's look and timing, and its subject slot takes what the user's line names, drawn in that look and arranged as that subject needs, as the Motion graphics item of [What to copy](../SKILL.md#what-to-copy) says. A template the row marks unresolved is the closest the preset came, and is copied the same way.
- A footage beat moves when the reference's does. A row marked moving plays moving footage: a stretch of the user's own recording from another moment, as a root video clip on its own track (`src="public/source.mp4"` with its `data-media-start`, as the [composition contract](../../aip-composition/SKILL.md) says for footage), or a researched clip the same way, with the row's tint drawn by a moment over it, a translucent ground. Only a row marked still takes a still image, the user's frame or a researched one, under a push. A frozen frame of the user's video is never a moving beat: several in a row read as a slideshow, so at most one beat of the hook is still. Compare the beat once against its keyframe.
